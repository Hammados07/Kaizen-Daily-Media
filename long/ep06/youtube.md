# Half a degree breaks a sunflower: why 137.5°

**Title (under 60 characters):** Half a degree breaks a sunflower: why 137.5°

**Description:**

A sunflower builds its head one seed at a time, and every new seed turns by the same amount from the last one. At 137.5 degrees the head fills with no gaps. At 137 degrees, dark gaps open between 21 curling arms. At a neat 144 degrees, the seeds fall into five straight spokes. In seven minutes you will see why the best turn is the one that is hardest to write as a fraction, and where the famous Fibonacci spirals come from.

The numbers come from the Rimi Lab golden angle lab with 500 seeds. The score is the worst ring: the gap from each seed to its nearest neighbour, against a perfect honeycomb (1.000), averaged ring by ring. A third of a turn (120°): 3 spokes, 0.037. Two fifths (144°): 5 spokes, 0.062. Three eighths (135°): 8 spokes, 0.099. 137.00°: 0.563 and 21 arms (137° is close to 8/21 of a turn, 137.14°). 137.51°: 0.884, with 34 and 55 spirals near the rim. 137.6°: 0.522 and 34 arms (close to 13/34 of a turn, 137.65°). With 1,000 seeds at 137.51°: 55 and 89 spirals, still 0.884. The golden angle is 360° ÷ φ² = 137.508°, where φ = 1.618 is the golden ratio.
