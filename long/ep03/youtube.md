# The space station is always falling

**Title (under 70 characters):** The space station is always falling | Orbits explained

**Description:**

Four hundred kilometres up, Earth's gravity is still 89% as strong as it is on the ground. Yet the astronauts on the space station float, and so does their water. Gravity did not go anywhere. In five minutes you will know what is really happening up there, why a lower orbit is a faster one, and why you slow down to catch the station.

The numbers come from the Rimi Lab Cosmos orbit lab, which works out a circular orbit from its height using Earth's GM (398,600 km³/s²) and radius (6,378.137 km) from NASA's Earth fact sheet. At 400 km: 92.56 minutes per orbit, 27,607 km/h, 15.56 orbits a day, gravity 89% of the ground. At 280 km: 90.11 minutes. At 2,000 km: 127.20 minutes. One turn per sidereal day (23.93 hours) at 35,786 km. In one second at 400 km the station moves 7.67 km sideways and falls about 4.3 m, the same 4.3 m the Earth curves away.
